\chapter*{Abstract}
How far can a compact, well-conditioned neural correlator go on top of an exactly antisymmetric Slater reference; what does a \emph{message-passing} architecture add over a separable one; and can such states be trained without Monte-Carlo sampling? We study these questions for interacting electrons in two-dimensional harmonic quantum dots across \(N\in\{2,6,12,20\}\) and trap frequencies \(\omega\) from \(1\) down to \(10^{-3}\), spanning the Fermi-liquid–to–Wigner-molecule crossover.

Our ansatz is a Slater–Jastrow state made \emph{safe by construction} for Laplacian-based objectives: \(\sqrt{\omega}\) coordinate scaling (trap units), \emph{soft-core} pair features with vanishing radial derivatives at coalescence, and an \emph{analytic} electron–electron cusp. On top of the Slater determinant, a neural correlator \(W_\theta\) reshapes the amplitude while a coordinate backflow reshapes the nodes; each can be built without message passing (a single feed-forward pass over per-particle and pairwise features, as in a DeepSet) or as a copresheaf \emph{message-passing} (CTNN) network. We train by brief residual preconditioning followed by stochastic reconfiguration (SR), and, separately, by importance-sampled, MCMC-free collocation. To interpret the trained states we read their variational tangent space through the quantum geometric tensor and the neural tangent kernel, summarised by an effective dimension \(d_{\rm eff}\).

Energetically, the ansatz matches diffusion Monte Carlo (DMC) within uncertainty for \(N{=}2\) and reaches \(\sim10^{-2}\!-\!10^{-1}\%\) relative error for \(N\in\{6,12,20\}\) wherever a DMC reference exists (\(\omega\ge0.1\)); below that, no DMC benchmark is available for \(N\ge6\), and we report agreement against our own converged variational energies and the expected Wigner energy scaling. Within the investigated regime, conditioning (trap units, analytic cusp, safe features) appears at least as important as network depth for accuracy.

Read through the tangent kernel, message passing supplies a single mechanism—a \emph{relational channel} that conditions each particle on the others. In the correlator it compresses the ground state onto roughly half as many effective tangent directions as a separable network; in the backflow it sustains a full-rank displacement field where a conventional, single-round-message backflow collapses to a single collective mode (rank \(\to1\)) at the Wigner crystal and loses accuracy. Both effects are invisible in the total energy until weak confinement turns the geometric deficit into an energetic one. The optimiser and training-paradigm choices follow the same geometry: SR helps in proportion to how ill-conditioned the tangent metric is—negligibly for \(|\Psi|^2\)-sampled VMC, decisively for collocation—and MCMC-free collocation matches VMC when its importance sampling is healthy and, with a physics-informed proposal built from the Wigner shell, reaches a genuine Wigner molecule at \(\omega=0.0035\) with no Monte-Carlo sampling during training.

Physically, we provide, to our knowledge, the first topology-resolved bond-orientational and stiffness diagnostics for parabolic dots across the crossover, with shell-mixture reconstructions of \(g(r)\) reaching cosine similarity \(0.989\!-\!1.000\) to the global histogram. At \(\omega=10^{-3}\) the \(N{=}6\) dot is a single-ring Wigner molecule with a robust \((1,5)\leftrightarrow(0,6)\) near-degeneracy (radial mode \(r_{\rm mode}\!\approx\!64.3\,a_0\)); the \(N{=}12\) dot is a two-shell molecule with a modal but non-exclusive \((3,9)\) split; and the \(N{=}20\) dot forms a three-shell molecule that collapses toward a single shell within one decade in \(\omega\). The unifying methodological lesson is that what separates architecture, optimiser, and training paradigm is not raw capacity but alignment with the low-dimensional, physically collective structure of the problem—the same structure that, in real space, is the Wigner molecule \cite{Egger_1999,Mazars_2008,Kong_2002,schweigert1994spectralpropertieschargedparticles,Filinov_2001,manninen2007metalclustersquantumdots}.
