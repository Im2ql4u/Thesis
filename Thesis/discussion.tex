\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}
\endgroup

Architecture, optimiser and sampling measure looked at the outset like three separate
choices, and they turned out to be one geometry. Architecture fixes the shape of the
variational tangent space, message passing compressing it in the correlator and holding it
open in the nodes. The optimiser inverts the metric on that space, which earns its cost
only while the metric is both badly conditioned and still measurable. And the sampling
measure decides what the metric is estimated from in the first place: $|\Psi|^2$ conditions
itself and stays on target, whereas a fixed covering proposal loses the conditioning first
and the state afterwards.

That leaves the question of what any of it establishes. The energies agree with an external
reference over part of the grid and with nothing at all over the rest, and the two
situations license quite different claims. Several of the internal quantities that make the
architecture comparison vivid are not properties of the state. The Markov-chain-free route
works inside a boundary whose location we had to find by crossing it. And the picture, if
it is right, says something about systems larger than these.

\section{What the energies establish}
\label{sec:energy-discussion}

The energies are the least interesting result in this thesis and the most necessary one.
Every claim downstream---about what the network learned, about the structure read off the
trained state---collapses if they do not hold. The question, then, is not whether the
numbers are good but what having good numbers actually buys, and the answer differs at the
two ends of the grid.

At the benchmarked end it buys less than it appears to. Agreement with a fixed-node DMC
calculation is agreement with another approximation. Where our message-passing ansatz sits
\emph{below} a published DMC value, as it does for $N{=}12$ at $\omega{=}1$, the natural
reading is that a better nodal surface has been found rather than that something has gone
wrong, since the variational principle bounds the energy from below by the exact ground
state and not by the nodal constraint of the reference calculation. We adopt that reading,
though establishing it would require the DMC trial nodes and a matched uncertainty
analysis, which we have not done. Stripped of interpretation, what remains is that two
independent methods agree to a few hundredths of a percent wherever both exist, and that is
the strongest external statement available anywhere in this work.

At the unbenchmarked end it buys more than it appears to, though only by inheritance, and
the inheritance has to be argued rather than assumed. Below $\omega=0.1$ no published value
exists for $N\ge6$. What carries across the boundary is not accuracy but conditioning: the
same architecture, protocol, trap-unit scaling, soft-core features and analytic cusp,
applied across three decades of $\omega$ without adjustment. A method retuned at each
confinement would carry nothing. This one carries its own behaviour, and the energies it
produces join the benchmarked regime smoothly and then follow the classical scaling
exponent. The deep-Wigner numbers therefore deserve to be taken seriously as variational
predictions, which is a weaker thing than being validated, and we do not call them
validated anywhere.

\section{How far the internal numbers can be pushed}
\label{sec:repr-discussion}

Nothing obliges a trained network to organise itself in a way anyone can interpret.
High-dimensional function approximators are entitled to be incomprehensible, and usually
are. What is surprising here is less that the internal organisation can be read than that
it is so small, and small along the grain of the physics rather than of the
parameterisation: a real-space transition involving shells, ring occupancies and angular
stiffness, held on one to three effective directions whose identity changes across the
crossover while their number does not.

That invites being read as a discovery about neural wavefunctions, and it cannot quite be
one, for a reason that reaches well past this thesis. The Jastrow and the backflow are
partial substitutes, so any measurement made on one of them alone---its rank, its
displacement magnitude, its share of the energy---records a division of labour that the
loss barely constrains and that a different training route could have settled differently
while arriving at the same state. Such quantities are gauge-like. Reporting them is
legitimate; treating them as properties of the architecture is not. Every claim that
reversed during this project was of that kind.

What survives a change of training route is what can be measured on the state itself or
by deleting part of it: the energy, the local-energy variance, the price of removing a
component, and the overlap between two trained states. On those the controlled comparison
of Chapter~\ref{ch:kernel} does support an architectural claim, and a sharper one than the
dimension count that first suggested it. Counting directions tells us how many the
correlator uses; the tangent-kernel analysis tells us which, and that turns out to be the
substantive question. The message-passing correlator's dominant directions are the
Hamiltonian's own collective modes---breathing, correlation hole, relative excitation---to
$R^2\approx1$ across $\omega$ and up to $N{=}12$, whereas a separable network of matched or
larger capacity leaves nearly half its tangent budget where no compact physical dictionary
finds it. A direction outside the dictionary need not be unphysical, so the defensible
version of the claim is that one tangent space is far more completely accounted for by a
small set of physical operators than the other. The gap persists at every confinement, and
strong confinement merely disguises it, correlation being a small slice of the energy there
and the two networks agreeing to four figures.

The practical upshot is a design rule rather than a theorem. For parabolic dots a
compact smooth correlator with a moderately wide backflow is a sound default: the
correlator behaves like a learned set of collective coordinates, while the backflow acts as
a local correction channel that switches on where the near-field physics is delicate and
falls quiet in the Wigner limit. The \emph{kind} of backflow keeps mattering, however, long
after its energy contribution has gone, since a conventional single-round network collapses
onto one collective mode exactly where the copresheaf network does not, and pays half a
percent for the collapse. Falling quiet and falling over are different things.

\section[Where MCMC-free training stops]{Where Markov-chain-free training stops, and why}
\label{sec:collocation-discussion}

``An accurate many-body wavefunction trained with no Markov chain'' is the kind of
sentence that travels further than its evidence, so the boundaries have to be drawn before
the claim is made. The collocation programme also consumed the most time and produced the
most failures of anything here, and the two facts are related: the method works inside a
regime whose edges had to be found by walking off them.

\paragraph{What ``MCMC-free'' does and does not mean.}
Three distinctions are easily conflated.
(i)~The pipeline is \emph{Markov-chain-free}: no configuration is ever drawn from a
Markov chain during training---samples come from an analytic proposal $q(\mathbf R)$ and
are importance-reweighted. It is not free of Monte Carlo in the broader sense;
importance sampling and multinomial resampling are Monte-Carlo operations themselves.
(ii)~It is not, in general, \emph{reference-free}. Where a DMC energy exists
($\omega\ge0.1$), the residual pretraining anneals its target toward $E_{\rm DMC}$ and
the instability rollback monitors the relative error against it, so the DMC value enters
the training signal. Those runs are trained without a Markov chain; they are not an
unsupervised solution of the Schr\"odinger equation.
(iii)~The deep-Wigner cascade is the one regime that is both Markov-chain-free
\emph{and} reference-free: below $\omega=0.1$ no DMC value exists, so the cascade is
guided by the variational objective alone and cross-checked against the fitted Wigner
scaling curve, never against an external benchmark.

\paragraph{Two walls, of different kinds.}
Most of the failures lie on a single chain of causes. Proposal--target mismatch inflates
the tail of the importance weights; the inflated tail destroys the gradient estimator; a
destroyed estimator makes the optimisation seed-dependent and the empirical metric
unreliable; and inverting an unreliable metric, which is what natural-gradient
preconditioning does, amplifies the noise instead of the signal. Langevin refinement joins
that chain at the first link, by breaking the proposal the weights are computed against.
CG-SR joins it at the last, failing at low $\omega$ because the metric can no longer be
estimated rather than because it is ill-conditioned. Even the $N{=}20$ reversal, where a
Jastrow-only ansatz beats the backflow it should in principle beat, arrives at the same
chain through memory instead of confinement, the edge tensor forcing the collocation budget
down until the gradient degrades. Everything on that chain is a sampling problem, and a
better proposal moves it: rebuilding the proposal from the Wigner shell pushed the wall
from $\omega\approx0.01$ down to $\omega=0.0035$.

Below $0.0035$ the obstacle changes character. It is no longer the sampler. Metropolis
walkers initialised directly on the classical shell relax inward regardless, the backflow
displacement transfers correctly and is simply far too small to bridge the gap, and the
collapse is non-monotonic across proposal variants in a way that a sampling failure would
not be. What fails is the warm start. At these confinements the Wigner peak exists only
because the Jastrow amplifies an exponentially suppressed determinant back up by a
comparable factor, and a $23\%$ change in $\omega$ perturbs both sides of that
cancellation, tipping the balance so that the density falls back to the non-interacting
shell. The ansatz is not covariant enough in $\ell$, which is a representational
limitation, and no improvement to the proposal will remove it.

Distinguishing the two matters practically, because it says where effort should go. The
general lessons survive the distinction in any case. Any importance-sampled scheme applied
to a weakly confined many-body system will meet the first chain in that order, and any
second-order method applied to one must certify that the metric it inverts is statistically
resolved at the sample sizes in use. Neither statement is about quantum dots.

\paragraph{Where the two routes actually stand.}
Compared directly against the SR route, collocation is competitive for
$N\lesssim12$ and $\omega\gtrsim0.1$, and loses ground below that. At $N{=}6$,
$\omega{=}1$ the two are indistinguishable, both inside DMC uncertainty. At $N{=}12$,
$\omega{=}0.1$ collocation is a few times worse. At $N{=}20$ it is not competitive at
all. The SR pipeline has its own scaling problems---mixing times grow with $N$ and with
correlation strength---but they arrive later, because an MCMC sampler adapts to
$|\Psi|^2$ throughout training while a fixed proposal cannot. The natural reading is
that the two are complementary rather than rival: collocation for fast, parallel,
chain-free exploration and pretraining; SR for final accuracy at large $N$ or weak
confinement.

\section{What the picture predicts}
\label{sec:implications}

If the argument of this thesis is right, it should hold well outside the quantum dot, and
in three particular ways.

\emph{Up to the frontier, the proposal is the bottleneck rather than the objective.}
That makes the next move a better sampler rather than a better loss---normalising flows
trained against $|\Psi|^2$, or the physics-informed proposals of
\S\ref{sec:kernel-q3-frontier} generalised beyond ring geometries. Rebuilding the
proposal from the Wigner shell lifted the effective sample size by $15$--$47\times$
without any training at all. Past the frontier the target changes: what is needed there
is an $\ell$-covariant parametrisation, or an objective anchored on the shell
\emph{amplitude}, so that a converged state survives being carried to a weaker trap.

\emph{Relational structure will matter more, not less, with $N$.} The tangent-space gap
between message-passing and separable correlators widens with particle number in the
range we can measure---$\lesssim0.02$ against $\approx0.77$ dictionary-orthogonal mass at
$N{=}12$. If that continues, the architectures that scale are the ones that maintain a
transported edge representation rather than pooling pairwise features and discarding
them.

\emph{The quadratic backflow is the first thing that has to change.} The
$\mathcal{O}(N^2 h_{\mathrm{bf}})$ edge tensor is what stops the method at twenty
electrons, and it stops it for reasons of memory rather than of physics. Sparse or
low-rank message passing, or a local backflow that avoids all-to-all edge features,
would extend the reach without touching anything else in the pipeline---and, on the
evidence of Chapter~\ref{ch:kernel}, without giving up the nodal correction that is the
reason for having a backflow at all.

Finally, a caution that applies to this thesis more than to any of its predictions. The
architecture comparison is cleanest at $N{=}6$, where it is seeded; the $N{=}12$ contrast
rests on fewer trained models and the $N{=}20$ analysis on a single production state.
The tangent-space picture is therefore established at one size and indicated at the
others. Extending it is not a matter of interpretation but of compute.
