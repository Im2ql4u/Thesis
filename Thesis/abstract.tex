\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

A neural network used as a variational wavefunction is judged by its energy. This thesis
asks what the energy cannot see: two networks landing on the same number need not be the same
object, a preconditioner decisive in one training regime does nothing in another, and an
objective that reproduces a Markov chain's answer in one corner of the phase diagram departs
from it in the next. The system is the two-dimensional harmonic quantum dot, from the
kinetic-energy-dominated droplet to the dilute Wigner molecule, for $N\in\{2,6,12,20\}$
electrons and confinements $\omega$ from $1$ to $10^{-3}$.

The ansatz is a Slater determinant dressed by a neural Jastrow correlator, which reshapes the
amplitude, and a coordinate backflow, which reshapes the nodes. Each exists in a separable
form and a message-passing one. Training is by residual pretraining followed by stochastic
reconfiguration, and separately by importance-sampled collocation that places no Markov chain
in the gradient. The trained states are read through the geometry of their
log-derivatives (the quantum geometric tensor and the neural tangent kernel) and through
structural diagnostics of the electron arrangement. Energies reproduce published fixed-node
diffusion Monte Carlo wherever it exists, to $0.0008$--$0.04\%$; below $\omega=0.1$ for
$N\ge6$ none exists, and those states are predictions.

Message passing changes the variational geometry before it changes the energy. At strong
confinement a message-passing correlator reaches the same reference-quality solution along
about half as many effective tangent directions as a separable network of equal or greater
capacity, and at every confinement far more of its tangent weight lies inside a compact
dictionary of collective operators. The same advantage appears in the nodal channel: toward
the Wigner regime a conventional backflow collapses spectrally to rank one and loses about
half a percent in energy, whereas the message-passing field retains its rank. Holding the
backflow fixed and swapping only the correlator moves the collapse boundary, which points to
one relational bottleneck rather than two deficits.

Natural-gradient preconditioning earns its cost where the tangent metric is anisotropic and
still \emph{estimable}. Conditioning alone is not the criterion: under $|\Psi|^2$ sampling the
empirical metric is badly conditioned on exactly the models where stochastic reconfiguration
and Adam agree to four decimals.

The trained states then serve as instruments, and the crossover they resolve is
\emph{staged}: the cloud expands and its shells stiffen radially well before the electrons
acquire angular order within them. A polygon shell model fitted on held-out frames reproduces
the measured pair distribution far better than an angle-randomised null, and at
$\omega=10^{-3}$ the twelve-electron dot forms a two-shell molecule with a modal $(3,9)$
split while the twenty-electron dot collapses from three shells to a single ring within one
decade in $\omega$.

Those measurements return to the solver. A generic collocation proposal loses the Wigner
state entirely; a proposal rebuilt from the measured shell geometry lifts the effective
sample size by $15$--$32\times$ with no training at all and carries Markov-chain-free training
to $\omega=0.0035$, where a different limit takes over---a transfer instability in the
Slater--Jastrow cancellation that no change of proposal repairs.

Two limitations qualify these results. Every state is the lowest found within a fixed
spin-projection sector, and total spin is not scanned. The architecture comparison is seeded
at $N{=}6$, rests on fewer models at $N{=}12$ and on a single production state at $N{=}20$,
and the two backflow variants are not capacity-matched.
