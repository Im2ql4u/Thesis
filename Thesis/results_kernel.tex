\chapter{Architecture, optimiser, and paradigm through the tangent kernel}
\label{ch:kernel}

The preceding chapter established that our ansatz --- a Slater determinant dressed
by a message-passing (CTNN) Jastrow and coordinate backflow --- reaches
diffusion-Monte-Carlo (DMC) quality across the $(N,\omega)$ grid, and that its
internal feature space is low-dimensional and reorganises with the physical
regime (\S\ref{sec:repr-analysis}). This chapter asks \emph{why}, and does so
by comparing the three design axes of a neural quantum state against each other
through a single lens.

\paragraph{Three questions.} We treat three choices as co-equal, first-class
questions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{Q1}] \textbf{Architecture.} When and why does a message-passing
  correlator (CTNN) beat a separable, pairwise one (a permutation-invariant
  DeepSet)?
  \item[\textbf{Q2}] \textbf{Optimiser.} What does stochastic reconfiguration
  (SR, natural gradient) actually do to the update, and where does it beat Adam?
  \item[\textbf{Q3}] \textbf{Paradigm.} What changes between $|\Psi|^2$-sampled
  variational Monte Carlo (VMC) and importance-sampled collocation --- in
  conditioning, variance, and the learned state?
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{One lens.} Each question is asked through the geometry of the
per-sample log-derivative (score) matrix
\begin{equation}
  O_{ki} \;=\; \frac{\partial \log|\Psi_{\btheta}(\bx_k)|}{\partial \theta_i},
  \qquad k=1,\dots,B,\;\; i=1,\dots,P,
  \label{eq:kernel-O}
\end{equation}
whose two Gram matrices are the quantum geometric tensor (QGT / Fisher metric)
$S = O^{\!\top}O / B$ and the neural tangent kernel (NTK) $K = OO^{\!\top}$.
The central diagnostic is the \emph{effective dimension} of the variational
tangent space, the participation ratio of the QGT spectrum $\{\lambda_a\}$,
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{eff}}(S) \;=\; \frac{\left(\sum_a \lambda_a\right)^2}{\sum_a \lambda_a^2},
  \label{eq:deff}
\end{equation}
which counts how many independent tangent directions carry Fisher weight at the
solution, regardless of the parameter count $P$. We read it alongside the local-energy
variance $\Var(E_L)$ (zero only for an exact eigenstate, hence a measure of
wavefunction quality), the sampling efficiency (effective sample size, ESS), and
the condition number $\kappa(S)$.

\paragraph{Discipline.} Every cross-architecture $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ comparison
uses a \emph{common probe set}: all models are scored on the \emph{same}
configurations drawn from a fixed reference measure, not on each model's own
$|\Psi|^2$. We report $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ only
where it has converged in the sample count $B$ (the participation ratio, not the
sample-capped numerical rank), and we anchor the tooling on the analytic
two-electron ground state, for which the CTNN ansatz reproduces
$E = 3.000127$~Ha ($+0.004\%$) with squared overlap
$|\langle\Psi_{\btheta}|\Psi_{\mathrm{exact}}\rangle|^2 = 1.000000$.%
\footnote{Analytic $N=2$ solution at $\omega=1$: $E=3\,\hbar\omega$,
$\Psi\propto e^{-(r_1^2+r_2^2)/2}(1+r_{12})$; cusp $\mathrm{d}J/\mathrm{d}r|_0=1$.
% TODO cite Taut1993
} All statistics below are single-seed unless a seed count is stated.

\paragraph{Headline findings.} \textbf{(Q1)} A message-passing correlator compresses
the ground state into $\sim\!2\times$ fewer effective tangent directions than a
separable DeepSet at weak coupling ($1.40\pm0.17$ vs $3.25\pm0.03$ at $\omega=1$,
$\sim\!11\sigma$, seeded); the gap \emph{closes} toward the Wigner crystal, where
both reach $\sim\!3.7$ but occupy different tangent subspaces and CTNN's is the
lower-variance one. The tangent directions are \emph{named} at the exact $N=2$
anchor --- they are the physical collective modes of $H$ (breathing,
correlation-hole, relative excitation) to $R^2=1.00$. A single-seed dimension
``inversion'' at the crystal is reported and then \emph{retracted} under seeding
(\S\ref{sec:kernel-q1-sweep}), as a worked cautionary result. \textbf{(Q2)}
Stochastic reconfiguration whitens the tangent kernel (exact at $N=2$); its
advantage over Adam is null in the easy regime and first resolves at low $\omega$.
\textbf{(Q3)} The collocation--VMC difference is governed by the sampling
\emph{measure}: $|\Psi|^2$ is self-preconditioning, the covering proposal drives a
monotone ESS collapse, and the weak form discards the Laplacian's zero-variance.
Throughout, the robust cross-cutting discriminator is $\Var(E_L)$ --- lower for CTNN
at every $\omega$.

\section{Q1 --- Architecture: message passing compresses the correlator}
\label{sec:kernel-q1}

\subsection{At weak correlation, energy hides the difference; dimension reveals it}

At $N=6$, $\omega=1$ the two architectures are \emph{energetically
indistinguishable}. Trained to DMC quality, every model --- CTNN and DeepSet
alike, across a $20$k--$164$k parameter ladder --- lands within $\pm0.1\%$ of the
reference energy; the best raw energy in the whole set belongs to a DeepSet at
$164$k parameters ($-0.052\%$), \emph{twice} the CTNN parameter count. Total
energy is a blunt discriminator here: both ans\"atze have saturated the variational
floor.

The difference is in the \emph{geometry}. Table~\ref{tab:fair-dim} reports the
fair, common-probe effective dimension. Two facts stand out. First, the CTNN
reaches the ground state in a tangent space of
$d_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 1.2$--$1.7$ (three seeds), while every DeepSet needs
$d_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 2.9$--$3.6$ --- a factor of $\sim\!2$. Second, this gap
is \emph{robust to the probe measure} (the value moves by $\lesssim 0.2$ whether
scored on CTNN-, DeepSet-, or pooled samples) and \emph{does not shrink as the
DeepSet is given more capacity}: the $164$k DeepSet sits at $d_{\mathrm{eff}}=2.9$,
no lower than the $20$k one. The compression is architectural, not a matter of
size or of where each net places its sampling mass.

The lower variance travels with the lower dimension. Even where the energies tie,
the CTNN's $\Var(E_L)$ is consistently below the DeepSet's ($2.3\times$ at
$\omega=1$; Table~\ref{tab:omega-sweep}), i.e.\ the CTNN's wavefunction is closer
to a true eigenstate. This is the honest form of ``CTNN beats FFNN'': not in
energy, but in the \emph{quality} and \emph{compactness} of the represented state.

\subsection{Across $\omega$: CTNN's compression advantage is a weak-coupling effect}
\label{sec:kernel-q1-sweep}

The weak-correlation anchor tells only half the story. Sweeping $\omega$ from the
shell regime ($\omega=1$) toward the Wigner crystal ($\omega=0.01$) at $N=6$
(Table~\ref{tab:omega-sweep}, Fig.~\ref{fig:omega-sweep}) shows the CTNN's effective
dimension rising with correlation ($1.2$ at $\omega=1$ toward $\sim\!3.7$ near the
crystal) while the DeepSet's stays near $3.3$--$4.3$ throughout. The single-seed
sweep appears to cross over --- CTNN's dimension overtaking DeepSet's at
$\omega=0.01$ --- but, as we show next, that dramatic endpoint is a single-seed
artifact: seeded, the two dimensions \emph{converge} rather than invert. The robust
statement is milder and cleaner: CTNN compresses far below DeepSet at weak coupling
($1.2$ vs $3.4$), and that gap \emph{closes} toward the crystal.

The single-seed sweep suggests an \emph{inversion} at the crystal --- CTNN's
dimension overtaking DeepSet's --- but this is exactly the kind of dramatic
single-seed claim that must be checked before it is believed. Retraining three
seeds per architecture at $\omega=0.01$ to matched analysis-grade quality

a checkpoint/optimisation-path artifact: at $\omega=0.01$ the landscape is flat
enough that $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ depends on which ground state a run settles into, so a
single seed is not diagnostic there. What is robust and seeded is the
\emph{variance}: CTNN's $\Var(E_L) = 1.8\times10^{-5}$ vs DeepSet's

$+0.072\%$). So at the Wigner crystal the two architectures reach the \emph{same}
effective dimension, but CTNN finds a lower-variance state within it --- and, as the
cross-projection below shows, a \emph{different} tangent subspace.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The physical mode count is the same for both nets at
each $\omega$ (Table~\ref{tab:modecount}): it is set by the state and rises from
$\sim\!2$ to $\sim\!6$ toward the crystal. What differs is whether the \emph{tangent}
dimension follows it. CTNN's does, at a roughly constant fraction (ratio
$\approx0.5$--$0.6$): it allocates variational directions in step with the physics as
the mode count grows. DeepSet's does not --- pinned near $3.3$--$3.8$, its ratio
\emph{crosses unity} around $\omega=0.1$: over-complete at weak coupling (three
tangent directions for a $\sim\!2$-mode state, redundant), and converging to CTNN's
dimension only at the crystal. The architectural difference is therefore a
\emph{weak-coupling} one --- CTNN compresses where DeepSet is redundant --- and it
closes toward Wigner, where both reach $\sim\!3.7$--$3.8$ (seeded,
Table~\ref{tab:crossover-seeded}) and CTNN's remaining advantage is variance.

\paragraph{Whose collective coordinate?} A cross-architecture projection on the
common probe --- both nets' NTK eigenfunctions live in the same space of functions on
the probe points --- shows the two architectures differ not only in \emph{how many}
directions they carry but in \emph{which}. At weak-to-moderate coupling
($\omega\geq0.1$) they share the \emph{same single leading collective coordinate}:
DeepSet's top direction captures $\geq0.98$ of CTNN's and vice versa ($k_{90}=1$),
differing only in the redundant directions DeepSet carries on top. At the crystal (on
the matched-grade seeded checkpoints) their leading modes become nearly
\emph{orthogonal} (mutual top-1 capture $\approx0.01$): CTNN's leading mode needs
three DeepSet directions to represent, DeepSet's needs all six of CTNN's, and the
top-3 subspaces overlap only $0.42$. So at Wigner the nets reach the \emph{same}
effective dimension but span \emph{different} tangent subspaces --- and CTNN's is the
lower-variance one. The message-passing advantage thus changes character across the
sweep: dimensional \emph{compression} at weak coupling (a global collective
coordinate is a coordinated deformation across all particles, which iterated message
passing and the V-cycle's coarse-graining carry in one coherent direction, whereas a
sum of pairwise features must approximate it piecewise), and a better \emph{choice} of
subspace at the crystal, where the dimension is common. This reframes the earlier
feature-space observation (\S\ref{sec:repr-geom}): the correlator does not merely
\emph{live} on a low-dimensional manifold, it \emph{sizes} and \emph{orients} that
manifold to the physics.

\subsection{Naming the manifold}
\label{sec:kernel-naming}

What \emph{are} these tangent directions? At $N=2$, $\omega=1$ the ansatz is exact
(overlap$^2 = 1.00000$, $E = 3.00000$~Ha), so we can compare the network's Jastrow
tangent modes --- functions of the pair distance $r_{12}$ --- against the exact
physical responses of the Hamiltonian in the \emph{same} function space: the
breathing response $\partial_\omega J^\star$ (trap scale), the correlation-hole
response $\partial_\lambda J^\star$ (Coulomb strength $\lambda$), and the relative
excitations $\phi_n = u_n/u_0$ (exact excited states of the relative problem).

The identification is essentially perfect (Fig.~\ref{fig:response-projection}). The
network's leading tangent mode is captured by
$\mathrm{span}\{\partial_\omega,\partial_\lambda,\phi_1\}$ to \textbf{1.000}, and
its top-two subspace to $0.990$. A least-squares naming of the leading mode gives
$R^2 = 1.000$ with the breathing response dominant ($\partial_\omega$ coefficient
$-0.83$), plus the first relative excitation ($\phi_1$, $-0.16$) and negligible
$\partial_\lambda$/$\phi_2$. All four physical modes lie inside the network's
top-six span ($0.92$--$1.00$); the remaining network directions are
tiny-eigenvalue numerical modes with no physical content.

So the $\sim\!1$-dimensional variational manifold at weak coupling \emph{is the
breathing mode}, and the effective $2$--$3$-dimensional tangent space is exactly
$\{$breathing, correlation-hole, first relative excitation$\}$. This names the
dimensions of Table~\ref{tab:modecount} at the exact anchor and grounds the
physical reading the $N=6$ sweep only inferred: the directions the ansatz must
resolve are the low collective modes of $H$, and the tangent dimension is their
count. It also sets up Q2 --- the dominant, softest direction is the smooth
breathing, while the short-range correlation-hole (the cusp physics) is a stiffer,
subdominant direction, which is where the optimiser question turns.

\paragraph{Naming at $N=6$ by operator decomposition.} There is no analytic $N=6$
solution, so we name the tangent modes there against a basis of physical operator
generators evaluated on the probe --- monopole/breathing $\sum_i r_i^2$, quadrupole
$\sum_i(x_i^2-y_i^2)$, short-range correlation-hole
$\sum_{i<j}e^{-r_{ij}^2/2\sigma^2}$, pair-Coulomb $\sum_{i<j}1/r_{ij}$, and
pair-linear $\sum_{i<j}r_{ij}$. At $\omega=1$ the leading tangent mode of \emph{both}
architectures is a named physical collective mode --- a pair-correlation/breathing
combination ($R^2 = 0.99$ CTNN, $1.00$ DeepSet) --- so the $N=2$ picture carries to
$N=6$. At the Wigner crystal a sharp, seed-robust difference appears (three seeds
each): CTNN's leading mode stays physical ($R^2 = 0.93\pm0.02$, dominated by the
pair/Coulomb/breathing operators), while \emph{DeepSet's leading mode is not physical
at all} ($R^2 = 0.01\pm0.01$) --- its physical content is demoted to a subdominant
direction. This independently corroborates the cross-projection
(\S\ref{sec:kernel-q1-sweep}): at strong correlation the separable net's dominant
variational direction drifts off the physical collective modes, whereas message
passing keeps its leading direction oriented to the physics.

\subsection{Scaling in $N$ (at initialisation)}
\label{sec:kernel-q1-scaling}

Does the compression advantage grow with system size? The trained-state answer at
$N\geq12$ is blocked by training instability and exact-Laplacian memory (a separate
infrastructure task), but the \emph{architectural inductive bias} can be probed
directly and cheaply, because it is already present at initialisation (the $N=6$ gap
appears in the untrained tangent space). Building the untrained $f$-net tangent space
on a common Gaussian probe --- first derivatives only, no Laplacian --- gives the
effective dimension of the architecture's raw parametrisation (Table~\ref{tab:scaling},
three random inits each). CTNN's init dimension \emph{falls} with $N$
($2.1\to1.6\to1.4$ for $N=6,12,20$) while DeepSet's stays rigid ($\sim\!3.5$--$3.7$),
so the gap \emph{grows} with system size ($1.7\times\to2.6\times$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:dinit-scaling}). Message passing
compresses harder the more particles there are to correlate; a separable pairwise sum
does not. This is the inductive-bias dimension, not the trained-solution dimension
(the seed scatter at init is large, and init $\neq$ solution --- e.g.\ trained CTNN at
$N=6$, $\omega=1$ sits at $1.4$, below its init $2.1$); the trained-state $N$-scaling
remains for the deferred large-$N$ training campaign.

\section{Q2 --- Optimiser: what SR does, and where it earns its keep}
\label{sec:kernel-q2}

\subsection{SR whitens the tangent kernel (exact validation)}

Against the exact $N=2$ ground state, the role of natural gradient is
unambiguous. The plain (score-function) gradient weights each NTK mode by its
eigenvalue $\mu_a$, starving all but the few stiffest directions; SR reweights all
supported modes equally --- it \emph{whitens} the kernel. The physics residual,
$-(H-E)\Psi$ projected onto the tangent space, has power spread across \emph{all}
modes. The result is a near-orthogonality of the plain gradient to the imaginary-time
(Hamiltonian) flow that SR removes: at convergence,
$\cos(\delta\btheta_{\mathrm{SR}}, \text{$H$-flow}) = 0.997$ versus
$\cos(\delta\btheta_{\mathrm{plain}}, \text{$H$-flow}) = 0.041$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ntk-whitening}), with the gap driven by an extreme conditioning
$\kappa(S)\sim 10^{12}$ on a $d_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx1.2$ tangent space. This is
the descriptive claim ``SR = NTK whitening'', validated exactly.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{ntk_whitening}
  \caption{NTK whitening at $N=2$, $\omega=1$ (exact ground truth). The plain
  gradient (weighting $\propto\mu_a$) collapses onto the softest modes; SR spreads
  the update over all supported modes, matching the spectrum of the physics
  residual. This is what ``the gradient is misaligned with the Hamiltonian'' means
  concretely.}
  \label{fig:ntk-whitening}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Whether SR \emph{beats} Adam depends on the regime}

Descriptive whitening does not by itself imply a prescriptive advantage. To test
where SR actually beats Adam we ran a controlled experiment: a common Adam
warm-up, then a branch into Adam-only versus SR-only from an \emph{identical}
checkpoint, measuring against the exact state the energy error, the distance
$\|\log|\Psi_{\mathrm{exact}}| - \log|\Psi_{\btheta}|\|$, and its decomposition in
a fixed NTK eigenbasis split into stiff (low-$\mu$) and soft (high-$\mu$) halves.
Table~\ref{tab:sr-vs-adam} collects three regimes (four seeds each).

largely immaterial.} At $\omega=1$, with or without the cusp, SR and Adam tie

notably more reproducible: energy seed-s.d.\ $\approx0.025\%$ vs Adam

and the overall state, consistent with the interpretation that at low $\omega$ the
whole tangent space becomes ill-conditioned, not just a single cusp direction.

distance-to-exact: SR is $\sim\!10$--$20\%$ lower than Adam at every $\omega$ (the
less noisy signal). SR therefore helps, but modestly --- a slightly closer state
everywhere and a resolved energy edge around $\omega=0.1$, without a decisive,
monotonically growing advantage in the range tested. (The $\omega=0.01$ point is not
shown: the lightweight from-scratch diagnostic recipe diverges there; a genuine
SR-vs-Adam test at the crystal needs the warm-started, annealed cascade, left to
future work.)

\paragraph{Interpretation.} SR earns its keep where the ansatz and the measure leave
conditioning behind --- but with a physics-informed ansatz that residual is small,
and Adam's diagonal preconditioner reaches essentially the same floor. The honest,
seeded verdict is a \emph{modest, publishable} advantage: SR is consistently a little
closer to the exact state, with a resolved energy edge around $\omega=0.1$, rather
than a decisive win. The earlier single-seed claim that SR ``breaks the accuracy
wall'' at $\omega=1$ does not survive seeding and is withdrawn. In this ansatz the
optimiser is largely immaterial; the conditioning lives in the ansatz (cusp prior,
backflow) and the measure ($|\Psi|^2$) --- the through-line to Q1 and Q3.

\section{Q3 --- Paradigm: the sampling measure sets conditioning and variance}
\label{sec:kernel-q3}

\subsection{The collocation bottleneck is ESS collapse}

Collocation training replaces $|\Psi|^2$ sampling with an importance-weighted
proposal (a Gaussian mixture) that must \emph{cover} configuration space. We find
the dominant conditioning lever is not the strong-versus-weak form of the residual
but the \emph{sampling measure itself}. Under $|\Psi|^2$ the collocation operators
are mild ($N=6$, $\omega=1$: $\kappa(S)=6.6\times10^4$, weak-form
$4.7\times10^4$, strong-form $4.8\times10^3$); under the covering mixture the
\emph{same} operators exceed $\sim\!10^8$ --- a $\gtrsim\!2000\times$ penalty from
the measure alone.

The threshold-free diagnostic behind this is the effective sample size. As
$\omega$ falls, the broad proposal needed to cover the tails mismatches the
sharpening $|\Psi|^2$, and the importance-weighted estimator collapses onto a
handful of points (Fig.~\ref{fig:ess-collapse}): the ESS fraction runs
$3.0\%\to2.3\%\to0.59\%\to0.11\%$ across $\omega=1\to0.5\to0.1\to0.01$. At the
crystal only $\sim\!5$ of $4096$ draws carry the weight. VMC is, by construction,
free of this: $|\Psi|^2$ sampling gives unit weights, $\mathrm{ESS}=B$, and a
well-conditioned operator --- $|\Psi|^2$ is its own preconditioner.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{ess_collapse}
  \caption{Effective sample size of the importance-weighted collocation estimator
  versus $\omega$ ($N=6$, $4096$ draws). The monotone collapse to $0.11\%$ is the
  true collocation bottleneck; it explains why low-$\omega$ collocation needs
  continuation chains and adaptive resampling, and why natural gradient (which
  needs a stable Fisher estimate) degrades there.}
  \label{fig:ess-collapse}
\end{figure}

\subsection{The proposal frontier: conditioning versus unbiased coverage}

Given that the measure is the lever, how good is the trainer's ``smart'' adaptive
mixture? Comparing single Gaussians against the adaptive mixture
(Fig.~\ref{fig:proposal-compare}) shows the mixture sits \emph{off} the efficient
frontier: at $\omega=0.1$ a matched single Gaussian achieves $\mathrm{ESS}=1.6\%$
and a resolved $\kappa(A)=1.5\times10^5$, versus the mixture's $0.67\%$ and
$9.6\times10^7$ --- roughly $600\times$ better conditioned and $2\times$ more
efficient. But the matched Gaussian wins partly by \emph{not covering the hard
region}: its tail reaches $4\,\ell$ while the density needs $9.5\,\ell$ at
$\omega=0.1$ (and $22\,\ell$ at $\omega=0.01$); only the mixture's broad
components reach there. The real trade-off is thus \textbf{conditioning versus
unbiased coverage}: good conditioning bought by ignoring the tails risks a
solution that is wrong where it was never penalised.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{proposal_compare}
  \caption{Proposal comparison at $N=6$. Every proposal with $\mathrm{ESS}\gtrsim1\%$
  has a resolved $\kappa(A)\sim10^5$; every one below $1\%$ floors at $\sim\!10^8$
  (effective rank $\approx$ ESS). A matched Gaussian dominates the adaptive mixture
  on both conditioning and efficiency, but only by sacrificing tail coverage.}
  \label{fig:proposal-compare}
\end{figure}

\subsection{The Laplacian's gift is zero variance --- the weak form discards it}

Finally, the weak (Rayleigh) form and the strong (local-energy) form estimate the
same energy but differ enormously in variance. At the exact $N=2$ ground state the
strong estimator has $\Var(E_L)=1.4\times10^{-3}\approx0$ (the zero-variance
principle: $E_L$ is constant on an eigenstate), while the weak estimator has
$\Var(e_w)=2.9$ --- a ratio of $2071\times$. The same ordering holds for trained
$N=6$ states (Table~\ref{tab:zero-variance}). The means agree within the (large)
weak-form sampling error, consistent with the integration-by-parts identity
$\langle E_L\rangle = \langle e_w\rangle$: the weak form is unbiased but a
catastrophic estimator. This is why the winning recipe keeps a forward-only,
detached local-energy reward and a small weak-form weight.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The three collocation results are one statement: what
makes an estimator well-conditioned and low-variance is $|\Psi|^2$ sampling plus
the Laplacian. VMC has both for free; collocation trades them for coverage, and
must claw back stability with continuation, resampling, and ESS control. The
practical claim in the literature (and in an earlier draft of our own method
notes) that the weak form ``eliminates the conditioning catastrophe'' is
\emph{contradicted} here --- under the covering measure the weak-form operator is
just as ill-conditioned as the strong one. % (run_weak_form.py header claim corrected in commit a582d56)

\subsection{Dual-track: the two paradigms reach the same state, at different quality}
\label{sec:kernel-q3-dualtrack}

Does collocation reach a different \emph{wavefunction} than VMC, or the same state by
a noisier route? At the exact $N=2$ anchor we train the identical CTNN (same
initialisation) two ways --- VMC ($|\Psi|^2$-MCMC, strong-form $E_L$) and weak-form
collocation (a fixed Gaussian proposal, importance-reweighted Rayleigh energy, never
sampling $|\Psi|^2$) --- and compare both to the exact state and to each other
(Fig.~\ref{fig:dual-track}). Both converge to essentially the \emph{same}
wavefunction: overlap$^2$ with exact is $0.99998$ (VMC) and $0.99920$ (collocation),
and the two trained states overlap each other at $0.99918$; the learned pair
correlation $u(r)$ overlays the exact curve for both. The paradigms do not find
different states --- they find the same ground state. What differs is \emph{quality}:
collocation's $\Var(E_L) = 5.1\times10^{-2}$ is nearly $5\times$ VMC's
$1.1\times10^{-2}$ (a marginally worse eigenstate), and its weak-form energy read-out
settled at $2.94$, below the exact $3.0$, \emph{even though the state itself was
accurate}. That last point is the zero-variance principle made concrete: the weak
estimator is unbiased in principle but noisy enough (here at a benign
$\mathrm{ESS}\approx37\%$ for $N=2$) to mis-report the energy while the wavefunction
is right. The effective dimension is the same for both ($\approx1.1$), as it must be
for the same state. So the collocation--VMC distinction is not about \emph{which}
state is found but about the variance at which it is resolved --- exactly the
measure/Laplacian story of the preceding subsections.

\section{Synthesis and discussion}
\label{sec:kernel-synthesis}

The three questions converge on a single object: the trained wavefunction lives in
a \emph{low-dimensional, cusp/kinetic-dominated tangent space}, and architecture,
optimiser, and paradigm are each about how faithfully they resolve it.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Architecture (Q1)} sets \emph{how many} collective directions the
  ansatz spends and \emph{which}. Message passing compresses hard at weak coupling
  (tangent $d_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx1.2$ vs DeepSet's $\approx3.4$, a $\sim\!2$-mode
  state); toward the Wigner crystal the two dimensions \emph{converge}
  ($\approx3.7$--$3.8$, seeded) as the physical mode count grows, but they occupy
  \emph{different} tangent subspaces and CTNN's is the lower-variance one. So the
  CTNN advantage is dimensional compression at weak coupling and, everywhere,
  wavefunction quality ($\Var(E_L)$ lower at every $\omega$) --- not energy.
  \item \textbf{Optimiser (Q2)} sets whether that space is \emph{navigable}. SR
  whitens the QGT (exactly validated); it is immaterial where the ansatz already
  removes the stiff directions (weak coupling, cusp on) and earns its keep where
  $\kappa(S)$ and $\Var(E_L)$ grow (low $\omega$), where its energy advantage over
  Adam first becomes statistically resolved.
  \item \textbf{Paradigm (Q3)} sets whether that space is \emph{resolvable with low
  variance}. $|\Psi|^2$ sampling and the Laplacian deliver conditioning and
  zero-variance for free; collocation sacrifices both for coverage, and its
  bottleneck is a clean, monotone ESS collapse.
\end{itemize}

The kinetic/zero-variance thread links Q1 and Q3 (the same $\Var(E_L)$ that
discriminates the architectures is the quantity the Laplacian drives to zero), and
the low-rank, ill-conditioned tangent space links Q1 and Q2 (a lower-dimensional
$S$ is both cheaper for SR to whiten and the reason plain gradients misalign).

\paragraph{Caveats.} The single-seed $\omega$-sweep (Table~\ref{tab:omega-sweep})
suggested a dramatic dimension \emph{inversion} at the crystal; seeded, matched-grade
retraining overturned it (Table~\ref{tab:crossover-seeded}: the dimensions converge,
$3.70\pm0.04$ vs $3.84\pm0.08$) --- the single-seed-promotion failure mode caught
before publication. The two seeded anchors are now solid: the weak-coupling gap is
confirmed from \emph{independent} runs (five CTNN, three DeepSet at $\omega=1$:
$1.40\pm0.17$ vs $3.25\pm0.03$, $\sim\!11\sigma$), and the crystal convergence from
three seeds each (these share an $\omega=0.1$ warm-start, so their bands are
measurement/optimisation variance, not independent basins from $\omega=1$ --- the
full seeded cascade is deferred). What is robust: the compression gap at weak
coupling, its closure at the crystal, the $\Var(E_L)$ discriminator (CTNN lower at
every $\omega$, seeded in both regimes), the cross-projection (leading modes shared
at weak coupling, orthogonal at the crystal), and the exact $N=2$ naming of the modes
(\S\ref{sec:kernel-naming}). Remaining limits: the $\omega=0.01$ natural-orbital count
is on a grid-truncated
1-RDM (trace $\approx1.9$ of $3$), so its absolute value is uncertain (the
cross-architecture comparison is fair, as the grid is shared); the mode-naming is
\emph{exact} only at $N=2$ --- at $N=6$ it is by operator decomposition
(\S\ref{sec:kernel-naming}), which names the leading mode but not a full basis; and
the $N$-scaling is measured only at \emph{initialisation} (Table~\ref{tab:scaling}:
the gap grows $1.7\times\to2.6\times$ to $N=20$), with the trained-state $N$-scaling
deferred behind the large-$N$ training infrastructure. The weak-form variance is
heavy-tailed, so only the order of magnitude of the zero-variance ratio is robust.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:kernel-conclusions}

Viewed through the tangent kernel, the three design axes of a neural quantum state
resolve into complementary statements about one low-dimensional variational
manifold.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A message-passing correlator compresses at weak coupling and finds a
  better state at the crystal; energy hides both.} At weak correlation, CTNN and
  DeepSet tie in energy, but CTNN reaches the state in half the effective tangent
  dimension ($\sim\!1.5$ vs $\sim\!3.3$, fair and capacity-independent) and with
  lower local-energy variance. Toward the Wigner crystal the compression gap
  \emph{closes} --- seeded, the two dimensions converge ($3.70\pm0.04$ vs
  $3.84\pm0.08$; the single-seed ``inversion'' did not survive) --- but the nets
  occupy \emph{different} tangent subspaces and CTNN's carries $1.8\times$ lower
  $\Var(E_L)$. And the manifold is \emph{named}: at the exact $N=2$ anchor the leading
  tangent direction is the breathing response ($\partial_\omega$) and the effective
  tangent space is $\{$breathing, correlation-hole, first relative excitation$\}$ to
  $R^2=1.00$ --- the dimensions are the low collective modes of $H$.
  \item \textbf{SR whitens the tangent kernel, but its prescriptive advantage is
  modest.} The whitening picture is exact at $N=2$; the advantage over Adam is null
  in the easy regime (with or without the cusp, which itself acts as a preconditioner)
  and becomes statistically resolved only around $\omega=0.1$, then washes back into
  the seed noise on energy at $\omega=0.05$--$0.03$ (SR stays consistently
  $\sim\!10$--$20\%$ closer to the exact state throughout). With a physics-informed
  ansatz the optimiser is largely immaterial --- conditioning lives in the ansatz and
  the measure, not the optimiser.
  \item \textbf{The sampling measure, not the residual form, governs collocation.}
  $|\Psi|^2$ sampling is self-preconditioning; the covering proposal collocation
  requires drives a monotone ESS collapse ($3.0\%\!\to\!0.11\%$) and a
  $\gtrsim\!2000\times$ conditioning penalty, and the weak form additionally
  discards the Laplacian's zero-variance property (a $10^2$--$10^4\times$ variance
  premium). VMC is the better-conditioned paradigm; collocation's value is in
  coverage, at a quantified cost. Trained side by side on the same $N=2$ ansatz, the
  two routes reach the \emph{same} ground state (mutual overlap$^2$ $0.9992$) ---
  collocation simply at $\sim\!5\times$ higher $\Var(E_L)$, with a weak-form energy
  read-out that mis-reports while the state is right. The paradigm choice is about
  the variance of resolution, not the identity of the state.
\end{enumerate}

Together these make the low-dimensional, kinetic-dominated tangent space the
organising object of the analysis: the architecture sizes it to the physics, the
optimiser navigates it, and the paradigm determines the variance at which it can be
resolved. The open frontier --- naming the manifold's directions as physical
response and excitation modes, seeding the crossover, and scaling the dimension
gap in $N$ --- is the subject of the next stage of work.
