\chapter{What the message-passing backflow learns}
\label{ch:backflow}

\section{Setup and the questions}

We study $N$ electrons in a two-dimensional parabolic trap of frequency
$\omega$ with Coulomb repulsion. The trial wavefunction is a closed-shell
Slater determinant times a PINN Jastrow times a coordinate \emph{backflow}
$\Delta\mathbf{x}$, which displaces each electron's effective coordinate as a
function of the whole configuration. We compare two backflow architectures,
identical in every other respect: a \textbf{conventional} per-particle backflow
(each $\Delta\mathbf{x}_i$ from particle $i$'s neighbourhood), and a
\textbf{message-passing (CTNN)} backflow, in which particles exchange learned
messages on a graph before their displacements are read out. The trap frequency
tunes the physics: at large $\omega$ kinetic energy dominates; as
$\omega\!\to\!0$ (the \emph{Wigner} regime) Coulomb dominates and the electrons
localise. We ask: \textbf{Q1 (architecture)} what does message passing let the
backflow learn that a per-particle backflow cannot? \textbf{Q2 (optimizer)} does
stochastic reconfiguration (SR, natural gradient) beat Adam? \textbf{Q3
(paradigm)} does weak-form collocation reach the same state as $|\Psi|^2$-VMC?
A tangent-kernel picture (score matrix $O_{ki}=\partial_{\theta_i}\log|\Psi(x_k)|$,
quantum geometric tensor $S=O^\top O/B$, effective dimension
$d_{\mathrm{eff}}=(\sum\lambda)^2/\sum\lambda^2$) is the common language.

\section{Q1: the conventional backflow collapses; message passing does not}
\label{sec:q1}

\subsection{Energies}
Both architectures reach reference quality at $\omega=1$ but diverge as the trap
weakens (Table~\ref{tab:bf_energies}): the CTNN backflow holds $\sim\!0.02\%$
error across all $N,\omega$, while the conventional backflow degrades to $+0.5\%$
at $N=6,\omega=0.01$ and $\sim\!10\times$ the CTNN error already at $N=20,
\omega=0.1$.

\subsection{The rank collapse}
Let the \emph{backflow rank} be the participation ratio of the displacement field
$\Delta\mathbf{x}$ (how many independent directions the backflow moves electrons
along). At large $\omega$ both use $\sim\!N$ modes. Toward Wigner the
\emph{conventional} backflow \textbf{collapses to a single collective mode}
(rank $\to 1$) while the CTNN backflow \textbf{retains full rank $\sim\!N$}
(Table~\ref{tab:bf_rank}); the collapse sharpens with size ($10\!\to\!1$ at $N=6$
becomes $22\!\to\!1$ at $N=12$). The tangent dimension confirms it: toward Wigner
$d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ grows for CTNN ($\sim\!4\!\to\!10$) and collapses for conv
($\sim\!6\!\to\!1$). At $N=20$ (run only to $\omega=0.1$) both hold rank $\sim\!36$,
i.e.\ the collapse $\omega$ was not reached---consistent with $N=6,12$, where conv
rank is intact at $\omega=0.1$ and collapses only at $\omega=0.01$.

\subsection{Why: what the backflow and the messages buy}
Ablating the backflow ($\Delta\mathbf{x}\!=\!0$, sampler re-optimised) and
splitting the recovered energy into kinetic and Coulomb parts gives two
seed-stable facts. (i) \textbf{A kinetic$\to$Coulomb crossover:} at $\omega=1$ the
backflow buys kinetic energy; at $\omega=0.01$ the gain is entirely Coulomb
($\Delta V_{\mathrm{Coul}}\!\approx\!+0.017$, $\Delta T\!\approx\!0$). ``Message
passing buys kinetic energy'' is a high-$\omega$ statement only. (ii) \textbf{At
Wigner the messages \emph{are} the backflow:} zeroing the inter-particle maps
($\rho_{v\to e},\rho_{e\to v}$) costs as much as deleting the whole displacement.
A per-particle backflow has no channel for relative positioning---exactly what the
crystal needs---so it collapses to rank~1; the messages supply that channel.

\section{Q2: SR helps exactly when the optimisation is ill-conditioned}
\label{sec:q2}

Varying only the optimiser from a common stage-3 base:
\textbf{for VMC, SR and Adam are indistinguishable} (exact at $N=2$, var
$\sim\!10^{-9}$; energy gap $\lesssim 0.002$ at $N=6$, both seeds)---the VMC metric
is well enough conditioned that Adam suffices. \textbf{For collocation, SR is
essential:} $E_{\mathrm{Adam}}-E_{\mathrm{SR}}$ is large and positive (e.g.\ $+2.9$
at $N=6,\omega=1$), because the importance weights make the objective badly
conditioned. The verdict is \textbf{SR matters precisely when the tangent metric is
ill-conditioned}---a function of $\kappa(S)$, not of the optimiser.

\section{Q3: paradigms agree for small systems, diverge as they grow}
\label{sec:q3}

We test whether collocation and VMC reach the same \emph{state} via the symmetric
overlap $\mathcal{S}^2=\langle\Psi_B/\Psi_A\rangle_{|\Psi_A|^2}
\langle\Psi_A/\Psi_B\rangle_{|\Psi_B|^2}$.
\textbf{$N=2$: identical states} ($\mathcal{S}^2>0.997$, every $\omega$, both
optimisers, both seeds)---the paradigm is efficiency-only, collocation paying
$\sim\!5\times$ the variance. \textbf{$N=6$: they diverge}---with Adam
$\mathcal{S}^2$ falls $0.85\to0.77\to\approx0$ from $\omega=1$ to $0.01$ (both
seeds), because the fixed proposal's ESS collapses to $0.11$ at $\omega=0.01$;
a degenerate estimator, not undertraining. SR partially rescues it
($\mathcal{S}^2\!\approx\!0.95$) but does not close the gap. Weak-form collocation
thus has a \textbf{domain of validity}: equivalent to VMC when small/weakly
correlated, degrading as $N$ and $1/\omega$ grow. This couples Q3 to Q2---the
paradigm that needs SR (collocation) is the one that breaks down at scale---and
motivates an adaptive proposal (future work).

\section{Synthesis: one tangent kernel, and a gauge caveat}
\label{sec:synthesis}

All three answers are statements about the tangent kernel: Q1 is a rank collapse
of the backflow's contribution to $O$; Q2 is the conditioning $\kappa(S)$ of the
metric the optimiser inverts; Q3 is whether two measures induce the same
$O$-column space. A caveat is itself a result: \textbf{component-level quantities}
(backflow rank, $|\Delta\mathbf{x}|$, per-component $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$) are
\textbf{gauge-like}---the Jastrow and backflow are substitutes, so how correlation
is split between them is a nearly-flat, path-dependent direction of the loss, and
those quantities depend on the training route, not the state. Every claim that
reversed during this project was of that kind. The invariant measures---ablate and
price in energy, or compare converged states by overlap---are stable, and are what
the conclusions rest on.

\section*{Conclusions}
The message-passing backflow's advantage is specific and mechanistic, not a uniform
``better network.'' At weak confinement the per-particle backflow can only express
a rigid collective shift (rank~1), lacking any channel for relative positioning,
and its energy degrades; the graph's messages supply that channel, keep the
displacement full-rank, and do so by capturing Coulomb (not kinetic) correlation,
the regime where it matters. The optimiser and paradigm choices are governed by the
same tangent-kernel conditioning: SR is needed only for the ill-conditioned
collocation objective, and collocation equals VMC only until its importance
sampling loses the target at scale.
