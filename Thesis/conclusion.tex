\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusions}

\chapterepigraph{All things are implicated with one another, and the bond is
holy; and there is hardly anything unconnected with any other thing.}{Marcus
Aurelius}{\textit{Meditations}, Book VII, \S9}

This thesis set out from a single question---what does it actually take to solve
the many-body Schr\"odinger equation with a neural network, and what has the network
understood once it has---and the most durable answers came from the places where the
attempt failed. The quantum-dot benchmarks served a
dual purpose throughout: they test the numerical stability and expressivity of the
Slater--Jastrow PINN+backflow ansatz, and they provide a controlled setting in which
to study Wigner-molecule formation and the internal representations learned by the
network. What follows separates what has been demonstrated from what has been
observed, and both from what has merely been made plausible; that separation is
itself one of the results.

On the \emph{energy} side, a single trap-unit-normalised architecture with soft-core
pair features and an explicit cusp reaches DMC-level accuracy for two-electron dots and
holds sub-$0.1\%$ relative errors for $N\in\{6,12\}$ across $\omega\in\{0.1,0.5,1.0\}$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:energy-discussion}). It extrapolates smoothly to $\omega=10^{-3}$, where
no DMC data exist and internal checks---scaling exponents, virial ratios, structural
diagnostics---carry the reliability of what are then variational predictions. These
accuracies come from a modest SR tail on residual-based pretraining, which says that
conditioning and inductive bias matter at least as much as raw sample count.

On the \emph{structural} side, the weak-trap states develop bona fide Wigner-molecule
structure rather than being vaguely ``strongly correlated'' dots
(Sec.~\ref{sec:wigner-molecule}). As $\omega$ falls, all three systems $(N=2,6,12)$ cross
over smoothly: radii and localisation ratios move toward large stiff configurations, the
energy partitioning approaches the classical virial form $V_{\rm int}\approx 2V_{\rm
trap}$, and shell structures and bond orders emerge that match classical Coulomb-cluster
topologies---a single rotating ring mixing $(1,5)$ and $(0,6)$ at $N{=}6$, a modal
two-shell $(3,9)$ molecule at $N{=}12$. Shell-resolved reconstructions of $g(r)$ recover
the global pair structure from shell geometry and combinatorics alone (a decomposition
check; sampling correctness rests on the independent restart and chain diagnostics).
Throughout, $N_\uparrow=N_\downarrow$ is held fixed and total-spin sectors are not
scanned, so these are statements about the studied spin-projection sector rather than a
claim to have settled the global spin ordering.

On the \emph{representation} side, that real-space richness is carried by a strikingly
simple latent geometry (Sec.~\ref{sec:repr-discussion}). The correlator occupies one to
two effective directions whose leading axes act as collective coordinates for global size
and radial fluctuation, with angular and topological information held in conditional
distributions on them; across regimes the leading axis changes identity in a
$\phi\leftrightarrow\psi$ recomposition rather than growing in dimension. The backflow
is by contrast a high-dimensional displacement field whose energetic leverage sits in
near-field configurations at intermediate traps. One earlier reading---that the optimiser
simply switches backflow off---proves architecture-specific and is corrected here: it is
the \emph{conventional} backflow that collapses to a single collective mode at the Wigner
crystal and loses accuracy, its single round of pairwise messages failing to sustain the
relative displacement the crystal demands, while the copresheaf message-passing backflow
holds full rank and its accuracy. How these internal numbers may be read is itself a
result: component-level quantities are gauge-like, so the load-bearing conclusions rest on
the invariants---energy, overlap, ablation---not on the ranks.

On the \emph{method} side, the tangent kernel connects these observations to the design
choices that produced them (Chapter~\ref{ch:kernel}). Message passing earns its cost by
supplying a relational channel: it compresses the correlator onto fewer effective tangent
directions and sustains a full-rank backflow where a separable network cannot, and at the
crystal the inter-particle messages account for essentially the entire backflow
contribution. The sharper statement concerns physical structure rather than dimension
count---message passing keeps the correlator's leading tangent modes aligned with the
Hamiltonian's collective modes, whereas a separable network reaches the same energy with
much of its tangent capacity along directions a compact physical dictionary does not
resolve. That gap is present at every confinement, disguised at strong confinement only
because correlation is then a small slice of the energy; and it appears coupled to the
backflow, since the correlator's loss of alignment and the backflow's rank collapse set in
at the same confinement and shift together under a correlator swap, supporting a common
relational bottleneck. Natural-gradient preconditioning helps in proportion to how
ill-conditioned the tangent metric is, so long as that metric stays estimable---little for
$|\Psi|^2$-sampled VMC, decisively for collocation, and harmfully once importance
sampling collapses. The same conditioning story explains why an MCMC-free,
importance-sampled scheme equals VMC while its sampling is healthy and, with a proposal
built from the Wigner shell itself, reaches a genuine Wigner molecule at $\omega=0.0035$
with no Markov chain in the gradient optimisation (checkpoint selection and final
certification use MCMC).

\paragraph{Limitations and outlook.} Three limits bound these conclusions. External
ground truth reaches only to $\omega=0.1$; below it the accuracy statements are internal
consistency rather than benchmarked error, and the deep-Wigner energies should be read as
variational predictions supported by physical scaling, not as validated numbers. The
architecture comparison is cleanest at $N=6$, where it is seeded; the $N=12$ contrast rests
on fewer trained models and the $N=20$ analysis on a single production state, so the
size-scaling of the tangent-space picture is indicative rather than settled. And the
message-passing backflow that carries the crystal is quadratic in particle number---affordable
at these sizes, but the first thing that must change to reach the hundreds of electrons where
the same relational structure should matter most. None of these undermines the central
finding; they mark where it has been established and where it remains to be pushed.

Taken together, these findings converge on a single theme. What makes the difference
across architecture, optimiser, and training paradigm is not raw capacity but alignment
with the low-dimensional, physically collective structure of the problem---the same
structure that, in real space, \emph{is} the Wigner molecule. A compact correlator
supplies learned collective coordinates; a message-passing backflow supplies the
relational corrections a single-round-message network cannot sustain; natural gradients and $|\Psi|^2$
sampling keep the optimisation pointed along those directions. The quantum-dot results
thus provide both a stringent validation of the method and a template for the same
ideas in more complex finite fermion systems, where the trade-offs between global
structure, local correlation, and representation are expected to recur.
