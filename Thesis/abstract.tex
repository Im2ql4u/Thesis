\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

A neural network used as a variational wavefunction is judged by its energy. This thesis
asks what the energy cannot see: two networks landing on the same number need not be the same
object, a preconditioner decisive in one training regime does nothing in another, and an
objective that reproduces a Markov chain's answer in one corner of the phase diagram departs
from it in the next. The system is the two-dimensional harmonic quantum dot, from the
kinetic-energy-dominated droplet to the dilute Wigner molecule, for $N\in\{2,6,12,20\}$
electrons and confinements $\omega$ from $1$ to $10^{-3}$.

The ansatz is a Slater determinant dressed by a neural Jastrow correlator, which reshapes the
amplitude, and a coordinate backflow, which reshapes the nodes. Each exists in a separable
form and a message-passing one. Training is by residual pretraining followed by stochastic
reconfiguration, and separately by importance-sampled collocation that places no Markov chain
in the gradient. The trained states are read through the geometry of their
log-derivatives (the quantum geometric tensor and the neural tangent kernel) and through
structural diagnostics of the electron arrangement. The energies agree closely with every
available published fixed-node diffusion-Monte-Carlo reference, to $0.0008$--$0.04\%$---an
agreement, not an independent validation, since the same references guide pretraining.
Below $\omega=0.1$ for $N\ge6$ no reference exists, and those energies are predictions.

Message passing changes the variational geometry before it changes the energy. At strong
confinement, and under the parametrisations used here, a message-passing correlator reaches
the same reference-quality solution with its Fisher weight concentrated in about half as many
effective directions as a separable network of equal or greater capacity; at every
confinement far more of that weight lies inside a compact dictionary of collective operators,
and its local-energy variance is lower. In the nodal channel, toward the Wigner regime, a
conventional backflow collapses spectrally to rank one and loses about half a percent in
energy while a larger message-passing field retains its rank; the two are not
capacity-matched, so this is an observation and not yet an ablation. Holding the backflow
fixed and swapping only the correlator moves the collapse boundary, which points to one
relational bottleneck rather than two deficits.

Conditioning alone does not decide whether natural-gradient preconditioning earns its cost:
under $|\Psi|^2$ sampling the empirical metric is badly conditioned on exactly the models
where stochastic reconfiguration and Adam agree to four decimals. We propose, as a hypothesis
the observed failures support but do not establish, that what matters is whether the metric
is still \emph{estimable} from a batch.

The trained states then serve as instruments, and the crossover they resolve is
\emph{staged}: the cloud expands and its shells stiffen radially well before the electrons
acquire angular order within them. A polygon shell model fitted on held-out frames reproduces
the measured pair distribution better than an angle-randomised null, by a margin that grows
as the trap weakens. At $\omega=10^{-3}$ the twelve-electron dot forms a two-shell molecule
with a modal $(3,9)$ split and the twenty-electron dot a three-shell $(1,7,12)$ molecule,
which by $\omega=10^{-2}$ the radial classifier reads as one shell about a central electron,
with no angular order.

Those measurements return to the solver. A generic collocation proposal loses the Wigner
state entirely; a proposal rebuilt from the measured shell geometry lifts the effective
sample size by $15$--$32\times$ with no training at all and carries training with no Markov
chain in the gradient to $\omega=0.0035$, where a different limit takes over---a transfer
instability in the Slater--Jastrow cancellation that no change of proposal repairs.

Two limitations qualify these results. Every state is the lowest found within a fixed
spin-projection sector and total spin is not scanned, which matters most in the Wigner
regime, where spin transitions are plausible. The architecture comparison is seeded at
$N{=}6$, rests on fewer models at $N{=}12$ and on a single production state at $N{=}20$, and
the two backflow variants are not capacity-matched.
